Answers for the Director

Seasonality

Worst: February (mean 141.9 µg/m³), December (138.8) and January (137.6). Best: July (27.1), then June (32.7) and August (36.9). February is about 5.2 times July.

Task 7 band chart, Task 6 heatmap right-hand column.

Anomalies

Highest: 8 Nov (320.0) and 18 May (245.0). Both are isolated one-day spikes, not part of the seasonal curve. On 8 Nov the neighbours are 155.9 (day before) and 115.0 (day after), so the spike is more than twice either. 18 May sits in a month averaging 59.9, about four times the monthly mean, with 50.9 the day before and 55.3 after. Possible causes (hypotheses, the data cannot prove them): crop-residue or waste burning, an industrial or landfill fire, a dust storm (18 May was a hot day, 32.4 °C), fireworks, or a temperature inversion trapping smoke for a day. The third highest, 24 Feb (237.8), is also a spike.

Task 2 labelled points, Task 8 daily line.

Guideline

355 of 356 recorded days (99.7%) were above 15 µg/m³. 188 days (52.8%) were above 75 µg/m³. Only one day met the WHO guideline. Out of all 365 calendar days the shares are 97.3% and 51.5%.

Task 2 chips.

Build-up

Fastest: January (31 new days), December (31), November (30) and February (28 of 28 days), since every recorded day in these months was above 75. The curve is steepest from January to February and again from October to December. It goes flat in June, July and August, with 0 new days, staying at 100 from the end of May until 1 September.

Task 3 area chart.

Comparison

Yes, the shape is the same (correlation 0.98) with the suburb about 20% lower on average. The suburb exceeded the city on only 5 days. The gap is widest in winter, with monthly mean gaps of 32.7 µg/m³ in February and 27.4 in January, and narrowest in July (5.2). The largest single-day difference is 24 Feb: city 237.8 against suburb 157.3.

Task 4 two-line chart.

Composition

Severe days (above 150) occur in Jan (9), Feb (8), Mar (1), May (1), Oct (2), Nov (5) and Dec (11), 37 in total. July has the most Low days (28). Month lengths differ for two reasons: February has 28 calendar days, and April, June, September and November have 30. March shows only 22 days because 9 days (12–20 March) are missing.

Task 5 stacked bars, with dashed outlines marking full month length.

Pattern

Mostly by month. Weekday averages run from 80.6 (Saturday) to 90.4 (Tuesday), a spread of under 10 µg/m³, while monthly averages run from 27 to 142. Weekends are only slightly cleaner. The few odd cells (February Monday 202, May Sunday 110, November Saturday 164) are the 24 Feb, 18 May and 8 Nov spikes, since each cell holds only four or five days. This points to weather and season (cold, still winter air, heating, burning) rather than a weekday traffic or factory schedule. Temperature and city PM2.5 have a correlation of −0.76.

Task 6 heatmap.

Spread

November has the widest gap: 80.9 to 320.0, a range of 239.1 µg/m³. May is next (31.4 to 245.0, range 213.6). A monthly mean hides this because one spike barely moves the average. November's mean of 129 looks like a steady "bad" month and says nothing about a 320 day.

Task 7 band chart.

Trend and data quality

Only a seasonal cycle. The 7-day average falls from about 140 in winter to a July low and climbs back. December's mean (138.8) matches January's (137.6), so the year ends where it began. One year of data cannot show a long-term rise or fall. 9 days are missing (12–20 March, sensor offline). A line drawn straight across would invent a smooth fall between 162.9 (11 Mar) and 144.0 (21 Mar) and hide that nothing was measured. Averaging across it would mix days from either side of an unobserved week.

Task 8 chart with shaded gap.

Zoom

Zoom into November. The 8 Nov peak is a single day: 155.9 the day before, 320.0, then 115.0 the day after. The full-year chart makes it look like part of the winter plateau. Zoomed in, each day has its own dot and the hover shows exact values.

Task 9, "Zoom to widest-spread month".

Chart choice

The pie of monthly averages could not show (1) order or direction: which month comes first, whether pollution is rising or falling, or that it peaks in winter and bottoms in July. It also could not show (2) extremes, gaps and thresholds: the 320 spike, the 9 missing days, or how many days broke 15 and 75. A pie also suggests the months are parts of one whole, which averages are not.

Recommendation

December and January. They are consecutive, every recorded day in both was above 75 µg/m³ (31 each, Task 3), and they have the most Severe days (11 + 9 = 20 of the year's 37, Task 5). Their means (138.8 and 137.6) are also among the highest (Task 7), and the 7-day average stays high through both (Task 8). February has the highest mean (141.9) but only 28 days and 8 Severe days, so it is a close third. If the committee's school calendar allows a swap, February is the alternative.
